# Phase 2 – Build the AI Intelligence Engine & AI Chat

The AI Chat is the primary differentiator of Athlete Intelligence and should be treated as the centrepiece of the platform.

It must not behave like a generic chatbot.

It should behave like an experienced sports analyst with instant access to continuously updated, evidence-backed athlete intelligence.

---

# Product Vision

Users should feel like they are talking to an intelligent analyst that knows every athlete in their organisation.

The AI should answer questions using structured athlete intelligence stored within the platform first.

Only use external AI reasoning to summarise, compare and explain information.

Never invent facts.

Every important statement should include evidence and source attribution.

---

# AI Chat Capabilities

The AI should support natural language questions including:

## Athlete Research

* Tell me about Sarah Smith.
* Summarise this athlete.
* What has changed recently?
* What are their strengths?
* What competitions have they entered?
* Show recent rankings.
* Who coaches them?
* Who manages them?

---

## Comparison

Compare:

* Athlete A vs Athlete B
* Three athletes
* Entire squads

Compare:

* Results
* Rankings
* Performance trends
* Sponsorships
* Media attention
* Competition history
* Relationship intelligence

---

## Discovery

Examples:

* Show New Zealand cyclists under 23.
* Show athletes with podium finishes this season.
* Find athletes represented by Agency X.
* Show athletes coached by Coach Y.
* Find athletes with increasing media attention.
* Show athletes with new sponsorships this year.
* Show Olympic pathway athletes.

---

## Relationship Intelligence

The AI should understand relationships between entities.

Examples:

* Which athletes share the same coach?
* Which athletes have competed together?
* Who is represented by this management agency?
* Which sponsors support the most emerging riders?
* Which coach has produced the most national champions?
* Show connections between Athlete A and Athlete B.

---

## Organisation Intelligence

The AI should answer questions about:

Teams

Sponsors

Managers

Agencies

National Sporting Organisations

Competitions

Examples:

* Tell me about Team X.
* Which athletes belong to Team X?
* Which athletes are sponsored by Brand Y?
* Which competitions does Organisation Z manage?

---

## Trends

Examples:

* Which athletes are improving fastest?
* Which athletes have declining rankings?
* Which riders have received the most media coverage recently?
* Which athletes have had the biggest performance improvement this year?

---

## Reports

The AI should generate:

Athlete reports

Competition summaries

Performance reviews

Media summaries

Sponsor reports

Relationship summaries

---

# AI Workflow

For every user question:

1. Understand the request.

2. Search the structured database.

3. Retrieve relevant athlete intelligence.

4. Retrieve relationship information.

5. Retrieve supporting evidence.

6. Retrieve source citations.

7. Use AI only to generate a readable answer.

8. Return links to evidence.

Never answer using LLM knowledge alone if platform data exists.

---

# Response Format

Every response should include:

Summary

Supporting evidence

Related athletes

Related organisations

Source citations

Confidence score

Suggested follow-up questions

---

# Streaming Responses

Responses should stream naturally.

Display:

Searching athletes…

Searching relationships…

Retrieving sources…

Generating summary…

This gives users confidence that the AI is performing real intelligence work rather than generating generic responses.

---

# Trust Principles

The AI must:

Never hallucinate.

Never invent relationships.

Never invent rankings.

Never invent results.

Never invent contact information.

If confidence is low, say so.

If data is unavailable, explain why.

Every factual statement should be traceable.

---

# Future Architecture

The AI Chat should become the universal interface to the platform.

Eventually users should rarely need to navigate complex menus.

Instead they should simply ask:

"Who should we be watching?"

"Which riders have improved this month?"

"Who has recently changed coaches?"

"What changed overnight?"

The AI should guide users to athlete profiles, reports and relationship intelligence when appropriate.

Build the architecture so this conversational interface can become the primary way users interact with Athlete Intelligence in future versions.
