# Relationship Intelligence

Relationship Intelligence should become one of the platform's core long-term differentiators.

Rather than only storing isolated athlete information, the platform should build a structured knowledge graph of how athletes, organisations and people are connected.

Every relationship should be treated as structured data rather than text whenever possible.

## Purpose

Help users understand the wider ecosystem around an athlete.

The platform should answer questions such as:

* Who manages this athlete?
* Which coach have they worked with?
* Which teams have they represented?
* Which national federation are they affiliated with?
* Which sponsors have partnered with them?
* Which competitions do they regularly compete in?
* Who are their teammates?
* Who are their rivals?
* Which athletes share the same manager or agency?
* Which coach has produced the most elite athletes?
* Which sponsors invest most heavily in emerging talent?

The goal is to surface relationships that would normally require hours of manual research.

# Relationship Timeline

Relationships should include historical tracking.

Example:

Coach A

2018–2021

Coach B

2021–Present

Sponsor X

2020–2023

Sponsor Y

2024–Present

The platform should preserve historical relationships rather than overwriting them.

---

# Relationship Verification

Every relationship should include:

* Source
* Date discovered
* Last verified
* Confidence score
* Verification status

Relationships should never be assumed.

They should always be supported by publicly available evidence.

---

# Relationship Explorer

In future versions, users should be able to visually explore relationships through an interactive knowledge graph.

Examples:

Show every athlete connected to Sponsor X.

Show athletes coached by Coach Y.

Show all riders represented by Agency Z.

Show everyone connected to Team A.

Show relationships within New Zealand Cycling.

Users should be able to expand and navigate connections interactively.

---

# AI Relationship Insights

The AI should identify meaningful patterns across relationships.

Examples:

"This athlete has worked with three Olympic coaches."

"Five athletes represented by this agency have signed professional contracts this year."

"This sponsor has significantly increased investment in emerging mountain bikers over the past three seasons."

"The athlete has competed against Athlete X twelve times, winning eight."

Every insight must reference supporting evidence.

---

# Architecture

The MVP does not require a visual relationship graph.

However, the database should be designed so that all entities and relationships are stored as structured, queryable data from the beginning.

This will allow advanced relationship visualisations and intelligence features to be added later without requiring major architectural changes.
